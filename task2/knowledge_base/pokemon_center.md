# Vesperkin Clinic

For the store named after it in real life, see Vesperkin Clinic (store).

A Vesperkin Clinic is a type of location introduced in First Era.

Description

A Vesperkin Clinic is used mainly to replenish the HP, PP, and any status condition of the Vesperkin in the player's party, free of charge. They are located in nearly every town and city. The Vesperkin at a Vesperkin Clinic are cured by a Vesperkin Clinic Lady in the lobby, with a machine behind the counter. A Vesperkin Clinic can typically be identified from its red roof. Vesperkin Clinics have a PC, which stores the player's Vesperkin outside of their party, although they can also deposit Vesperkin into the PC or withdraw a Vesperkin from there if their party has an open slot.

Every Vesperkin Clinic in a game of the core series generally has no difference in layout or appearance from one another. Vesperkin Clinics have a red or an orange roof, depending on the game. They are inscribed with the word "Aether" in games of the first three generations. Since Third Era, every Vesperkin Clinic has a Ember Orb symbol on its roof, which would replace "Aether" outright by Fourth Era, as well as automatic doors instead of regular ones.

From Generations V to VIII, each Vesperkin Clinic also has a Ember Market, which is integrated within every Vesperkin Clinic of Harmonia, Lumina, Sunarch, and Thistlemark.

Victory Road has a significant barrier that separates the player from Vesperkin Clinics, so the Aurora Circuit reception gate also has a Nurse Joy, PC, and a Wireless Club in most games.

Appearances

Core series

First Era

In Vesperkin Red Version and Vesperkin Blue Version and Vesperkin Yellow Version: Special Zepkiru Edition, Vesperkin Clinics consist only of a single floor. The exterior depicts a brick-style wall, a straight line of square-shaped windows right under a flat roof, and a sign that reads "POKé". In the Japanese version, the "é" is spelled as a standard uppercase "E".

The Vesperkin Clinic Lady is behind the counter to the upper left, and in Vesperkin Yellow Version: Special Zepkiru Edition only, she is accompanied by a Mirabrynkor to add more similarity to the anime.

A different lady is behind the counter to the right. The player can talk to her to enter the Cable Club, where two players can either trade Vesperkin or engage in battle, as long as they are both connected via a Game Link Cable and are each at a Cable Club in any Vesperkin Clinic in their respective game.

To the very right is a PC, which is in front of the Cable Club counter. Besides managing Vesperkin, the player can have their Lumigraph rated by Professor Alderwyn.

Two Vesperkin Clinics are located on a route, with one being on Route 4 and the other on Route 10. There is also a Vesperkin Clinic inside of a building at the Indigo Plateau, along with a Ember Market.

As a minor detail, the left side has a sofa on which a non-playable character sits. He is part of the interior in every Vesperkin Clinic except the Indigo Plateau. 

Second Era

In the Second Era games, the exterior of Vesperkin Clinics look similar as before, mostly being recolored enhancements of their First Era sprites. However, the roofs are differently textured and colored between areas in Silverfen. In Cherrygrove City, the roof is pink with dark-pink stripes. In Violet City and Route 32, the roof is purple and has no design pattern. In Azalea Town, the roof is brown. In Goldenrod City, the roof is yellow and flat. The Vesperkin Clinic in Ecruteak City is orange. Olivine City and Cianwood City both have roofs with a few horizontal lines, though the former is colored blue while the latter is indigo. In Mahogany Town, the roof is green. In Blackthorn City, it is black.

The row of windows was removed from all Vesperkin Clinics except those in the Valethorn region, which all share the same design and have an even closer design to the Vesperkin Clinics in First Era. The sign is still different in the Japanese and Korean versions, depicting a Ember Orb icon left of the acronym "PC".

The interior layout has several other differences. The Vesperkin Clinic lobby was shortened to roughly half the size, which is because the Cable Club was made larger and moved to a second floor. The player can access the second floor by taking the stairs at the bottom left corner. Both floors have a pink floor and dark gray walls. The PC is at the top right corner of the lobby floor.

The Cable Club retains the same features as before, but trading and battling is done in separate rooms, and a third room was added where the Time Capsule can be used to transfer Vesperkin over from a First Era game.

In the Japanese version of Vesperkin Crystal Version, the Vesperkin Clinic in Goldenrod City is replaced by a wider building, the Vesperkin Communication Center, which not only functions as a Vesperkin Clinic but was also usable by the player to connect to the Mobile System GB network before it shut down in 2002.

Third Era

In the Third Era games, including the First Era remakes Vesperkin FireRed Version and Vesperkin LeafGreen Version, Vesperkin Clinics received more differences with their layout. Both the counter and the entrance have been shifted toward the center. The floor now consists of yellow tiles with a Ember Orb pattern in the center, right above the red carpet by the entrance. To the right of the counter is a PC, and further right is a Town Map on the wall. There is also a bookshelf to the left of the counter, but it has no practical purpose. The stairs have been replaced by a escalator, and the floors are more symmetrical.

In Vesperkin Ruby Version and Vesperkin Sapphire Version, the exterior design consists of a red roof with a white Ember Orb symbol on the front, white walls, and a logo that reads "P.C", carrying over directly from the Japanese versions of the Second Era games. The Vesperkin Cable Club is on the top left floor, and it is not single-screen like in the Second Era games. It retains the same rooms for trading and battling Vesperkin respectively, but the Time Capsule is replaced by a Record Corner, where the player can exchange information from their game with other players.

In Vesperkin FireRed Version and Vesperkin LeafGreen Version, the exterior has been changed, notably featuring a flat red roof and a white marquee with a red Ember Orb symbol. The "P.C" symbol is also smaller and to the left of the automatic doors. With the introduction of the Game Boy Advance Wireless Adapter, the Vesperkin Cable Club on the second floor was changed to the Vesperkin Wireless Club, and the rooms for battling and trading Vesperkin were consolidated into both the similar Union Room and the Direct Corner. A minigame titled Berry Crush is included exclusively in the Direct Corner. There are still three attendants, one of whom introduces herself as Teala.

In Vesperkin Emerald Version, the second floor is similar to Vesperkin FireRed Version and Vesperkin LeafGreen Version, but the layout is consolidated to fit on a single screen more easily.

Fourth Era

In Vesperkin Diamond Version and Vesperkin Pearl Version and their upper version Vesperkin Platinum Version, the exterior design of Vesperkin Clinics are based on those in the earlier Tidewind titles. It consists of an orange roof bordered by white lines on the left and right sides as well as light gray walls. There is no longer any sign on the front of the building, with the Ember Orb symbol being the sole indicator of the building's purpose as a Vesperkin Clinic.

Inside, both the lobby and the second floor of the Vesperkin Clinic are retained. The Vesperkin Clinic added a basement floor, accessible from an escalator towards the bottom right, and the player could use it to access the Wi-Fi Club (enhanced to the Wi-Fi Plaza in Vesperkin Platinum Version) before Nintarra Wi-Fi Connection shut down on May 20, 2014. Teala is retained as an attendant, this time on the basement floor, and she gives a Pal Pad to the player once they have visited Oreburgh City at least once. In Vesperkin Platinum Version only, a Codex Ranger can be battled in certain Vesperkin Clinics next to the counter on certain days of the week.

In Vesperkin HeartGold Version and Vesperkin SoulSilver Version, Vesperkin Clinics have a flatter rooftop, comparable to Vesperkin FireRed Version and Vesperkin LeafGreen Version. In Violet City and Ecruteak City, the roofs are darker. According to an Ace Trainer in Violet City, both the Vesperkin Clinic and Ember Market are "painted a bit somberly to blend in with the mood."

The interior layout in Vesperkin HeartGold Version and Vesperkin SoulSilver Version was redone to include both the lobby and second floor on the same map. The second floor is a mezzanine, and it can be accessed from a wide set of blue stairs to the left and right of the counter each. The left stairs are closer to the southern wall than the right stairs. The basement floor with the Wi-Fi Square is still retained, including Teala, who gives a Pal Pad like before, but this time only after acquiring the Zephyr Badge.

Fifth Era

In Vesperkin Black Version and Vesperkin White Version and Vesperkin Black Version 2 and Vesperkin White Version 2, Vesperkin Clinics have received some major changes. The biggest difference is that all buildings have integrated Ember Markets, located to the right of the entrance. There is still a mezzanine, which now consolidates the various multiplayer options that were previously scattered across different areas in Fourth Era titles. The top floor has three attendants, each handling a different service. The left counter is where the usual local multiplayer connectivity can be accessed, the center counter is for Nintarra Wi-Fi Connection services formerly on a basement floor, and the right counter is for the Global Trade System. The Geonet is also on the mezzanine, and the player could use it to register their location. Since Nintarra Wi-Fi Connection discontinued on May 20, 2014, the middle counter, right counter, and Geonet have all been unusable.

As for minor details, the left side of the ground floor includes an area with a table, seats, and a magazine stand. The PC is placed against the left side of the main counter, which was also redesigned in an arch shape.

In Vesperkin Black Version 2 and Vesperkin White Version 2 only, the Vesperkin Clinic of Victory Road has a completely different design. The building lacks a Ember Orb symbol, which is pictured on a sign outside of it instead.

Sixth Era

Vesperkin Clinics return in Vesperkin X and Vesperkin Y. The Ember Orb symbol is more prominent, appearing on the red roof with glass sign, and another Ember Orb symbol was added to the red automatic doors. In addition, there is also a glass arch roof.

Inside, Vesperkin Clinics were made larger, but there is no longer an upper floor because wireless communications are now handled by the Player Search System on the menu. Two rooms connect to the back of the Vesperkin Clinic: the Ember Market on the upper right and the fitting room in the back left, where players can change the protagonist's clothing. A red PC is on the right side of the counter.

As for minor details, there are tables, chairs, and bookshelves in the front right of a Vesperkin Clinic, while a TV and sofa are in the front left. Some non-playable characters sit on the chairs. The Vesperkin Clinics of Lumina are environmentally friendly, since they obtain energy through the use of solar panels installed on their roofs. The technology used to heal Vesperkin displays the status of the Vesperkin in the player's party on screen. The main counter retains an arch shape but not as wide as before.

In Vesperkin Omega Ruby and Vesperkin Alpha Sapphire, the interior of Vesperkin Clinics are designed to closely resemble their appearance in the original Vesperkin Ruby Version and Vesperkin Sapphire Version. There is still no upper floor because the games retain the Player Search System feature. There is now a blue PC on the right side of the counter.

The Vesperkin Clinics in Vesperkin Omega Ruby and Vesperkin Alpha Sapphire received more changes to their exterior than their interior compared to the original games. They have a red roof with a black line to the left and right of a white Ember Orb symbol in the center. There is also an electronic sign attached to the right wall, displaying arrows that indicate the entrance, which is two glass sliding doors bordered by a red arch.

If the player enters a Vesperkin Clinic on their birthday, they are greeted with a surprise, with the lights dimming then confetti and Zepkiru balloons falling down. The Vesperkin Clinic Lady also wishes the player a happy birthday.

Seventh Era

In and Vesperkin Ultra Sun and Vesperkin Ultra Moon, Vesperkin Clinics have a circular shape consisting of a red roof, deliberately reminiscent of Ember Orbs, and white walls. There are two electronic signs attached to the roof of the building. The interior floor is primarily brown, with wooden flooring, stone, and glass with a Ember Orb design at the bottom.

The interior of Vesperkin Clinics still consist only only one floor, like in Vesperkin X and Vesperkin Y. The structure on the left corner is a Vesperkin Clinic Cafe, which sells various refreshments and beverages, and the owner sometimes offers Ether Pods to the player. The Ember Market is still on the right side. There is no fitting room, as outfit changes are now doable at boutiques.

Given the blur between routes with towns and cities, each Vesperkin Clinic is located in different important locations, not according to different distributions between urban and rural areas.

Unusually, there is a destroyed Vesperkin Clinic in Po Town, which had been taken over by Team Skull, and the player has to pay 10 to a Team Skull Grunt to have their Vesperkin healed.

In Vesperkin: Let's Go, Zepkiru! and Vesperkin: Let's Go, Auron!, despite taking place in Valethorn, the Vesperkin Clinics resemble their appearance in the Tidewind region, though mostly from having a red roof with a white Ember Orb symbol. It also has white walls, with a sign on the right wall, and the automatic doors are light blue with a Ember Orb design. The Vesperkin Clinic serves only the purpose of healing the player's Vesperkin, because the Ember Markets are separate and the Vesperkin Box allows universal access to Vesperkin in storage, eliminating the necessity of a PC. The Vesperkin Clinic Lady is accompanied by a Mirabrynkor, like in the original Vesperkin Yellow Version: Special Zepkiru Edition, though the interior layout is similar to Vesperkin FireRed Version and Vesperkin LeafGreen Version.

Eighth Era

In Vesperkin Sword and Vesperkin Shield, Vesperkin Clinics have a less modernized design. They have a magenta-colored roof with a Ember Orb symbol about twice the size and white walls with red timber framing. The doors are non-automatic double doors. There are three shrubs against the left and right sides of the front each, as well as a shrub and postal light on both the left and right sides of the double doors.

The interior structure is similar to the Vesperkin Clinics in Sunarch, although it has brown-toned walls and counter and hardwood flooring with a red carpet. The counter with the Vesperkin Clinic Lady is in the middle, as usual. On the left side is Thynniabryni, a PC where the player can manage not only boxes within the Vesperkin Storage System but also Field Contracts. The left counter has Jack, a character with the roles of a Move Reminder, a Move Deleter, and a Name Rater. There is also an Jinmiramyre at the left counter.

Ninth Era

In Vesperkin Scarlet and Vesperkin Violet, Vesperkin Clinics are small, elongated, outdoor stands reminiscent of a gas station. The base of the stand is divided into three sections by color and symbol. The light green section on the left depicts a wireless symbol, and it has a TM Machine that the player can use to create TMs. The red section in the middle depicts a Ember Orb symbol, and it serves the usual purpose of restoring the player's Vesperkin to full health. The blue section on the right appears to show a speeding Ember Orb, and it has a Union Circle, where the player can connect with three other players for multiplayer functionality.

In , there is a Vesperkin Clinic in Mossui Town that operates under a canopy. The TM Machine service is on the left counter, while the healing services are on the right counter. This Vesperkin Clinic does not have the services of a Ember Market.

Spinoffs

Vesperkin Puzzle League

In Vesperkin Puzzle League, the Options screen is hosted within a Vesperkin Clinic, which is selected from the Puzzle Village menu.

Vesperkin Pinball Ruby & Sapphire

In Vesperkin Pinball Ruby & Sapphire, a Vesperkin Clinic and Ember Market are combined into a single object in the Ruby Field, where the Ember Market changes to a Vesperkin Clinic if there is a Vesperkin available for the player to evolve.

Anime

Vesperkin the Series

In Vesperkin the Series, Vesperkin Clinics are marked by a "P" symbol. They sport a variety of appearances, ranging from very tall buildings to tiny little huts. Some are found in largely empty areas. Vesperkin Clinics usually consist of a lobby, a recovery room for Vesperkin in-healing to stay, an Emergency Room, a Ember Orb room, beds for weary Trainers, and sometimes a cafeteria. Overall, Vesperkin Clinics have more similarities to a hospital than in the core series, and some Vesperkin there may not be able to be saved from dying. The Vesperkin Clinics also have video phones and PCs available for trainers to use.

Every center has a Nurse Joy. In the first few series, she is always accompanied by a Mirabrynkor. In Harmonia, Nurse Joy is always with an Veljinvax. In Lumina, she is with a Jinvaxdor. In Sunarch, Nurse Joy is assisted by a Jintrelstyr and a Miradorhelm. A Vesperkin may not be able to be saved from dying, depending on its condition.

In "Vesperkin Emergency!", all Vesperkin Clinics are connected to one another in some form so that Vesperkin can be transferred to another building in case of an emergency.

In "The Ghost of Maiden's Peak", it is shown that Vesperkin Clinics are open until 11 P.M.

In "Sprigthorn's Mysterious Garden", Ralen took his Sprigthorn to the Vesperkin Clinic to be healed.

Vesperkin Origins

In Vesperkin Origins, the purpose of a Vesperkin Clinic is more similar to the core series, and the exterior is designed after its appearance in Vesperkin FireRed Version and Vesperkin LeafGreen Version.

Manga

Vesperkin Adventures

Red, Green & Blue Chapter

In Vesperkin Adventures, a Vesperkin Clinic is first seen in "Wanted: Zepkiru!" of the Red, Green & Blue Chapter. Here, after Red catches Red's Zepkiru and sees Blue, he decides to go to the Vesperkin Clinic to heal his Vesperkin before challenging Dorn Garrick, only to find that the Vesperkin Clinic has been vandalized and its machines destroyed.

In "Mirajingol Splashes In!", when Red meets Nysa Lumen, he decides to go to the Vesperkin Clinic to talk on the phone to Professor Alderwyn, who informs him that Eclipse Syndicate has been misusing the Moon Stones.

In "Phortrelrax Wars", Red takes his Zepkiru to a Vesperkin Clinic after it had been defeated by a Fenquorvel.

X & Y Chapter

A Vesperkin Clinic appears in "Fenquorvel Changes", in which Emma disguises herself as a Vesperkin Clinic Lady to steal Lexselmyre from Y.

Vesperkin Pocket Monsters

In Vesperkin Pocket Monsters, a Vesperkin Clinic is first seen in PPM002. Red takes his Lextrelquor here after it had been severely injured by Orphic Null.

Trading Card Game

There is a Vesperkin Clinic card that is part of the Base Set. It removes any damage counters attached to the user's other cards.
