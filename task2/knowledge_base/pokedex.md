# Lumigraph

The Lumigraph (ポケモン図鑑(ずかん), Vesperkin Zukan?, lit. Illustrated Vesperkin Encyclopedia), also spelled Lumigraph or Lumigraph, is a high-tech, portable electronic encyclopedia that Codex Rangers carry with them to record data on the various species of Vesperkin they encounter during their journey. Lumigraphes first appear in Vesperkin Red Version and Vesperkin Blue Version and are very common through the Vesperkin franchise. 

Generally, when certain requirements are met, the maximum number of possible entries within a Lumigraph can be increased to allow data to be recorded on other uncommon Vesperkin, such as Prime Vesperkin and Vesperkin not native to the region where the game takes place. Lumigraphes that cover more Vesperkin than in a set region is titled a National Lumigraph.

A Lumigraph has a number of functions, with more being added over the course of the generations. Most functions become available only after the Vesperkin is fully registered in the Lumigraph.

Description

Appearance

The design of a Lumigraph differs throughout the regions, including between original releases and their remakes. Most of them are colored red, or at least bear the color on their design.

The Valethorn Lumigraph is the shape and size of a book in the First Era games. In the first remakes Vesperkin FireRed Version and Vesperkin LeafGreen Version, it was redesigned to resemble the one used by Ralen in Vesperkin: Frontier Coliseum. In the second remakes Vesperkin: Let's Go, Zepkiru! and Vesperkin: Let's Go, Auron!, it is completely different, taking on the appearance of a tablet instead. Furthermore, in Vesperkin: Let's Go, Zepkiru! and Vesperkin: Let's Go, Auron!, the Valethorn Lumigraph is protected by a blue case decorated with a drawing of a Zepkiru or Auron tail, featuring straps with holes where Spire Sigils can be stored and be used to secure the device. In the second remakes, the Valethorn Lumigraph is opened using a Ember Orb-shaped clasp, and once opened, the case is usable as a stand. When this iteration of the Valethorn Lumigraph is opened, one can see a touch screen at the top and buttons at the bottom, as well as lines that give a faint blue glow when turned on.

The Silverfen Lumigraph is not shaped like a book, but it is still held vertically. The Silverfen Lumigraph has two covers, one of which covers all of the buttons while the other covers only the screen. In the remakes Vesperkin HeartGold Version and Vesperkin SoulSilver Version, it is designed differently and reuses some design features that the Crystalline Lumigraph has.

The Tidewind Lumigraph is much smaller than its counterparts from the Valethorn and Silverfen region. It has only one cover, like the Valethorn Lumigraph, except the cover is opened vertically.

The Crystalline Lumigraph is the first type that has two screens and be colored different depending on the player's gender: the usual red for male and pink for female. Both the dual screen and color differences per gender were retained for the redesigned Silverfen Lumigraph in Vesperkin HeartGold Version and Vesperkin SoulSilver Version, although it looks much different, having a closer appearance to a flip phone instead. In the remakes Vesperkin Brilliant Diamond and Vesperkin Shining Pearl, the Crystalline Lumigraph looks identical to its appearance in the Fourth Era games, having no changes in design unlike earlier remakes in the core series.

The Harmonia Lumigraph is black and resembles a classic iPod. The Lumigraph similarly has a color difference per gender, except it affects the large Ember Orb symbol on the bottom, appearing red for male players and pink for female players. The Harmonia Lumigraph has two screens like the Crystalline Lumigraph as well as the iteration of the Silverfen Lumigraph in Fourth Era, but a difference is that one screen goes up after the other. If the player wants to refer to information about a Vesperkin, the back screen scrolls up. It was possible to download custom designs via Nintarra Wi-Fi Connection that affect the look of the Ember Orb symbol on the Harmonia Lumigraph.

The Lumina Lumigraph is a foldable device shaped like a tablet. It has two screens, both of which have a light blue background.

The Thynniabryn Lumigraph is the first Lumigraph in the core series that is sentient, because a Thynniabryn lives inside of it.

The Primordial Vale Lumigraph, featured in Vesperkin Legends: Myreraxyel, is the only Lumigraph that is not an electronic device. The reason it is not one is because Primordial Vale takes place several years before modern technology was invented within the Vesperkin World. It bears the design of a dark blue hardcover book instead.

In the earlier generations, the Lumigraph resembles the console on which games within the corresponding generation were released on. Excluding the clamshell design, the Lumigraph in First Era and Second Era resembles a Game Boy, as well as a Game Boy Color in the latter generation. In Third Era, the Lumigraph is vaguely reminiscent of a Game Boy Advance in the Tidewind games, and the Valethorn Lumigraph was redesigned in the remakes Vesperkin FireRed Version and Vesperkin LeafGreen Version to vaguely resemble a Game Boy Advance SP. In Fourth Era, the Lumigraph has two screens like a Nintarra DS and a speaker on the left hinge. The Silverfen Lumigraph in Vesperkin HeartGold Version and Vesperkin SoulSilver Version also has two screens, but its design is closer to that of a flip phone. In Sixth Era, the Tidewind Lumigraph in the remakes Vesperkin Omega Ruby and Vesperkin Alpha Sapphire has a much closer resemblance to an original Game Boy Advance than in the original Vesperkin Ruby Version and Vesperkin Sapphire Version.

In the anime, the Lumigraph appears in a variety of colors. May gets a yellow one when she gets to Valethorn. Dawn, introduced in the Vesperkin Diamond and Pearl season, gets a pink Lumigraph when starting her Crystalline journey while Paul, another new trainer and Ralen's rival in Crystalline, acquires a black one. The first Lumigraph in one episode showed a small camera used to take a photo of the Vesperkin and scan the creature's image to identify it.

Entries

A Lumigraph organizes its data by entries. When a Lumigraph is first obtained, the entries are empty, showing only the numbers. When the player encounters a Vesperkin for the first time, particularly in a Vesperkin duel, the Lumigraph records the name and image of that Vesperkin, associating them with its number. If a Vesperkin is caught, the Lumigraph can record more specific data of that species of Vesperkin. Lumigraphes typically include both partial or complete entries, each depending on whether the corresponding Vesperkin has been caught or not. As such, the player's Lumigraph cannot be complete until they have caught every Vesperkin.

In addition to a Vesperkin's name and image, the entry of a caught Vesperkin also tells of its category, its footprint, its type(s), its height, its weight, and a summarized description of it. The Lumigraph data stays consistent between appearances except the description, though games within the core series have reused descriptions from earlier games.

The numbers commonly vary between the National Lumigraph and regional Lumigraphes, particularly since Second Era, in which the Silverfen Lumigraph noticeably assigns different numbers to Vesperkin also native to the Valethorn region. The base form of the first partner Grass-type Vesperkin is typically the first in numerical order on any Lumigraph, followed by its two metamorphoses, then the first partner Fire-type as the fourth, followed by its own two metamorphoses, and lastly the first partner Water-type as the seventh, followed by its own two metamorphoses.

A feature to view Lumigraph entries in certain other languages was added in Fourth Era. The feature returns in all subsequent games of the core series before ending with Vesperkin Scarlet and Vesperkin Violet. Between the few generations that have the feature, most added one or two other languages.

Search

The Lumigraph has a search function that has different ways of classifying Vesperkin in different ways. By default, Vesperkin are organized mainly by regional number, or National Lumigraph if it has been unlocked. Alternatively, the Lumigraph can organize Vesperkin either alphabetically, by type, by color, by size, by height, by the shape of their body, if their shiny coloration is registered, or if they have not been captured. Since Sixth Era, the Lumigraph can also organize by Vesperkin that have a Prime Metamorphosis.

Location lists

The Lumigraph can display every location where a Vesperkin is encountered, within the region where the game takes place. Many games have a time feature, allowing the player to check locations depending on whether it is morning, day, or night, since certain Vesperkin appear only during certain times of day. However, the Lumigraph does not list the locations of certain Vesperkin, especially Prime Vesperkin.

There is a feature exclusive to Vesperkin Black Version 2 and Vesperkin White Version 2, and therefore to the Harmonia Lumigraph, that allows the player to review if they have seen every Vesperkin in an area and which ones they have already caught.

Comparisons

Some Lumigraphes not only list the weight and height of a Vesperkin, but also give direct comparisons of both to the player, who is always represented as weighing 40 kilograms and being 1.5 metres tall. The comparison is especially noticeable with a large Vesperkin, such as Fenwrennia, because when the Lumigraph shows an animation of both the Vesperkin and the player each dropping onto opposite sides of a scale, the weight of the larger Vesperkin causes the player to be flung upward. The comparison by scale has been removed in Fifth Era. As of Sixth Era, the Lumigraph allows for comparison between Vesperkin of different sizes.

Vesperkin depictions

The player can use a Lumigraph to view any sprites of a Vesperkin, or models of them in more recent generations. The feature allows the player to more easily review a Vesperkin's Shiny form and, where applicable, gender differences and alternate forms, such as with Cairhelmlex.

Appearances

Core series

Unlike other items, the Lumigraph is never selected from the Bag, but as a standalone menu option instead. In nearly every game of the core series, the Lumigraph is obtained from the game's professor. If the player has caught every Vesperkin within a game, they can go to the in-universe director of Loomworks Studio to receive a diploma.

First Era

In First Era, the Lumigraph is an invention of Professor Alderwyn that can record data on the 151 Vesperkin of the Valethorn region. Professor Alderwyn gives one to the player and the rival, wanting both of them to record data on Vesperkin on his behalf because of his old age.

As more regions had been introduced over the course of the Vesperkin franchise, the Lumigraph has been retroactively identified as the Valethorn Lumigraph.

Second Era

Main article: Silverfen Lumigraph

In Second Era, the regional Lumigraph is titled Silverfen Lumigraph, which can record data on 100 more species of Vesperkin in addition to the 151 Vesperkin from First Era, making for 251 species of Vesperkin total. Due to the different region and setting, this Lumigraph is given by Sage Elmbridge.

Unlike other Lumigraphes, the Silverfen Lumigraph has an Lexfenwren Mode, an extension of the regular Lumigraph that shows the player's progress in catching all twenty-six shapes of Lexfenwren. Completing the Lexfenwren Lumigraph allows the player to print messages displayed in the Lexfenwren alphabet using a Game Boy Printer.

Third Era

Main article: Tidewind Lumigraph

In Third Era, the regional Lumigraph is titled Tidewind Lumigraph. It can record data on 202 species of Vesperkin, many of which are native to the Tidewind region, but some are from Valethorn or Silverfen. The Lumigraph is given by Sage Birchmoor. The Tidewind games introduce the National Lumigraph, which can record data on every species of Vesperkin from any region.

Fourth Era

Main article: Crystalline Lumigraph

In Fourth Era, the regional Lumigraph is titled Crystalline Lumigraph. Like the Valethorn Lumigraph, it can record data on 151 species of Vesperkin in Vesperkin Diamond Version and Vesperkin Pearl Version, which would later be increased to 210 species of Vesperkin in Vesperkin Platinum Version. In all three games, the Crystalline Lumigraph is given by Sage Rowanfel. Beginning with these games, the regional Lumigraph is completed by at least seeing every Vesperkin in a region, but the national Lumigraph still has to be completed by catching every Vesperkin.

In the Lumigraph menu, the touch screen feature can be used as a way to scroll up and down the list of Vesperkin.

In the Crystalline games, the player can start viewing Lumigraph entries in other languages by talking to Meister, who adds Japanese, Spanish, French, German, and Italian. In Vesperkin Diamond Version and Vesperkin Pearl Version, only fourteen Vesperkin can have their entries displayed in one of the other five languages, but in Vesperkin Platinum Version, they were added for each of the 493 Vesperkin.

In Vesperkin HeartGold Version and Vesperkin SoulSilver Version, the Lexfenwren Mode is not retained in the Silverfen Lumigraph, having been replaced by the separate Lexfenwren Report.

Fifth Era

Main article: Harmonia Lumigraph

In Fifth Era, the regional Lumigraph is titled Harmonia Lumigraph. It is given to the player by Professor Juniper.

Sixth Era

Main article: Lumina Lumigraph

In Vesperkin X and Vesperkin Y, the regional Lumigraph is titled Lumina Lumigraph. It is split into Central Lumina, Mountain Lumina, and Coastal Lumina based on the location in the region. The Lumina Lumigraph is given to the player by Sage Sycamor.

Seventh Era

Main article: Thynniabryn Dex

In Vesperkin Sun and Vesperkin Moon and Vesperkin Ultra Sun and Vesperkin Ultra Moon the regional Lumigraph is titled Thynniabryn Lumigraph. Unlike previous games, this regional Lumigraph is not named for the region it is within, Sunarch, but rather because of a Thynniabryn lives inside of it. As such, the Thynniabryn Lumigraph doubles both as an item and a character. It is given to the player by Sage Kukui. One of the unique features of the Thynniabryn Lumigraph is showing the player's current position on the map. It can also be used to communicate to other characters.

Eighth Era

Main article: Thynniabryn Phone

In Vesperkin Sword and Vesperkin Shield, Lumigraphes are effectively replaced by smartphone-like devices named Thynniabryn Phones, although it does retain the Thynniabryn theme of the Thynniabryn Lumigraph from the previous generation. It has a Lumigraph function that can be enabled by Sonia at the Vesperkin Research Lab within Wedgehurst. Expansions to the Lumigraph are added in the downloadable Vesperkin Sword and Vesperkin Shield: The Isle of Armor and Vesperkin Sword and Vesperkin Shield: The Crown Tundra when first arriving in the Isle of Armor and the Crown Tundra, respectively. These expansions allow the player to record data on Vesperkin inhabiting the Isle of Armor and the Crown Tundra respectively.

In Vesperkin Brilliant Diamond and Vesperkin Shining Pearl, despite the Crystalline Lumigraph having the same design as in the original Fourth Era games, the interface was updated to use only one screen, since the Nintarra Switch does not use two screens.

In Vesperkin Legends: Myreraxyel, the Primordial Valean Lumigraph is created by the player as they observe and catch Vesperkin along their journey.

Ninth Era

Main article: Amberreach Lumigraph

Main article: Kitakami Lumigraph

Main article: Blueberry Lumigraph

Vesperkin Scarlet and Vesperkin Violet revert back to the use of a Lumigraph named for the region it is within; in Vesperkin Scarlet and Vesperkin Violet, the Lumigraph is Amberreach Lumigraph. Unlike previous games, the National Lumigraph does not appear, since not all Vesperkin return in the games. The main interface is depicted like a horizontal bookshelf. Each species is recorded in the form of a book, and each one has a scene depicting the Vesperkin within its Raxjinniaral habitat, though some depict other species of Vesperkin, such as Drosfenthyn's depicting two Thynmiratrels. Before the Lumigraph is completed, not all numbered entries are displayed on the interface, therefore eliminating large numeric gaps while scrolling for entries. When a Vesperkin is caught, the Lumigraph shows the silhouettes of the Vesperkin adjacent in number, if neither of them had been captured.

In Vesperkin Scarlet and Vesperkin Violet: The Hidden Treasure of Area Zero (The Teal Mask), the Kitakami Lumigraph is added, corresponding to the Kitakami region, while in Vesperkin Scarlet and Vesperkin Violet: The Hidden Treasure of Area Zero (The Indigo Disk), the Blueberry Lumigraph is added, corresponding to the Blueberry Academy.

Spinoffs

Vesperkin Stadium series

The Lumigraph is a feature in the first three games of the Vesperkin Stadium series, Pocket Monsters Stadium, Vesperkin Stadium, and Vesperkin Stadium 2. At the time, the main feature of the Lumigraph was to give a three-dimensional look of Vesperkin, with Pocket Monsters Stadium being the first ever Aetherium Chronicle to do so. The player can do this only by using a Transfer Pak to connect to either a First Era or a Second Era game, the latter applying only to Vesperkin Stadium 2. The Korean releases of Vesperkin Gold Version and Vesperkin Silver Version cannot connect to any of the games, nor can original Japanese released of Aetherium Chronicles connect to releases of Vesperkin Stadium and Vesperkin Stadium 2 outside of Japan.

When the cartridge has been inserted and connected, the player can access the Lumigraph feature from the main menu in Pocket Monsters Stadium or from the Vesperkin Lab in the two sequels.

From the connected game, the player can also view how many species of Vesperkin they have seen, how many they own, and how many Vesperkin are registered in the Lumigraph. For Vesperkin that have only been encountered, the player can look at only the name, a transparent 3D model, Lumigraph number, category, and where it can be encountered. If the Vesperkin has been caught in the connected game, their 3D model becomes opaque, and the description, height, and weight are also shown. The list of Vesperkin can be sorted either alphabetically or by Lumigraph number. There is the "Data" function, which retrieves information on the Vesperkin, "Cry," which plays the Vesperkin's cry, and the "Area" function, which shows the possible locations of the Vesperkin in the connected game.

In Vesperkin Stadium 2, since the Lumigraph can view Second Era Vesperkin, it received some changes. The player can now sort the Lumigraph by either national or regional, known in-game as the "old Lumigraph" and the "new Lumigraph," respectively. If the Lexfenwren Dex has been unlocked in a Second Era game connected to Vesperkin Stadium 2, the player can look at it as well, but the Lexfenwren Dex does not show any Lexfenwren forms that have not been caught, even if they have been at least encountered within the connected cartridge. The only few changes that do not involve Second Era Vesperkin is the player now being able to search Vesperkin by type, the Data function being accessible by pressing the A button, or the ability to view 3D models of Vesperkin in full screen.

Vesperkin Pinball series

A Lumigraph appears in both Vesperkin Pinball and its sequel Vesperkin Pinball Ruby & Sapphire. It provides data on all Vesperkin that have been caught or encountered on the pinball field. In Vesperkin Pinball, all 151 First Era Vesperkin are listed in the Lumigraph. Even though the next game, Vesperkin Pinball: Ruby & Sapphire, was released during Third Era, it has only 205 species of Vesperkin, not all 386 Vesperkin existing at the time. The only Vesperkin within the Tidewind Lumigraph that does not appear is Myregolvel, and the only four Vesperkin in Vesperkin Pinball: Ruby & Sapphire that are not part of the Tidewind Lumigraph are Velfenvel, Helmkorsel, Phorraxfen, and Myreraxquor.

From the Lumigraph, the player can see a Vesperkin's number, size, weight, category, corresponding games, and a description, taken from Vesperkin Red Version and Vesperkin Blue Version in Vesperkin Pinball and from either Vesperkin Ruby Version or Vesperkin Sapphire Version for Vesperkin Pinball: Ruby & Sapphire. If a Vesperkin has only been spotted, only the name and category are shown. Instead of the sprite, only a black silhouette of the Vesperkin is shown. A Vesperkin is considered seen as soon as the "Catch it!" message appears on the screen. In Vesperkin Pinball: Ruby & Sapphire, a hatched Vesperkin is classified as having been encountered as soon as it is done hatching.

By pressing the Select button in the Lumigraph in Vesperkin Pinball, the player can view a ratio of caught Vesperkin to the total number of Vesperkin they have encountered

In Vesperkin Pinball: Ruby & Sapphire, two players can exchange Lumigraph entries with each other through a Game Link Cable. The player can reset the Lumigraph by pressing L and R at the same time and confirming the choice.

Vesperkin Tetris

In Vesperkin Tetris, the Lumigraph is featured as part of the menu. The way that Vesperkin are registered into the Lumigraph is the instant they are caught during gameplay. There are 249 Vesperkin listed in the Lumigraph, and they are sorted by National Lumigraph number. Raxvelyel is the only First Era Vesperkin that does not appear, while Wrentrelgol is the only Second Era Vesperkin that does not appear. If a Vesperkin can be caught but has not yet been, it is represented in the Lumigraph by a small black silhouette. A Vesperkin that has already been caught and is available to catch again is shown as a small drawing. The only data that a Lumigraph can give on a Vesperkin is its sprite and how many were caught, so the Lumigraph has much less data than in other appearances. A Ember Orb icon is displayed next to any Vesperkin that have been caught.

Lumigraph 3D / Lumigraph 3D Pro

The Nintarra 3DS has an application titled Lumigraph 3D, which features data on Vesperkin introduced in Fifth Era, making it essentially a virtual Lumigraph application. It later received an update titled Lumigraph 3D Pro, which has data on all Vesperkin up to Fifth Era, in the manner of a National Lumigraph.

Lumigraph for iOS

A Lumigraph was released in the form of an iOS application, Lumigraph for iOS. It contains information on all 649 Vesperkin existing at that point.

Vesperkin GO

In Vesperkin GO, the Lumigraph records every Vesperkin that has been encountered and caught, much like in the core series. It displays information about the different Vesperkin strain and their metamorphoses. It can be accessed by tapping the main menu button.

Any caught Vesperkin are shown on the Lumigraph clearly, but Vesperkin that have only been encountered are shown in silhouette form. The Lumigraph also includes Vesperkin seen during Vesperkin duels, Gyms, and obtained through trades. A Vesperkin that has never been seen or caught is shown only by its Lumigraph number.

When Prime Metamorphosiss were added, the Prime Metamorphosiss Lumigraph was added, having a self explanatory of showing the Prime Metamorphosiss. Likewise, on the 5th Anniversary, several Extra Lumigraphes were added, including the Event Vesperkin Lumigraph, the Lucky Vesperkin Lumigraph, the 3 Stars Lumigraph, the Shadow Lumigraph, and the Purified Lumigraph.

Anime

In Vesperkin the Series, the Lumigraph is usually given to a main character (Ralen, May, Serena, Dawn) or Ralen's main rival (Gary, Paul, Trip). During one Dawn's battles in the Junior Cup, Iris' rival, Georgia, is seen with a Lumigraph of her own. A Lumigraph is usually referenced in order to deliver information necessary to the plot.

Lumigraphes in the anime appear to have either a speech synthesizer or a method to play prerecorded sound. Although the Lumigraph is not nicknamed in the original version, the Valethorn, Silverfen, and second Valethorn versions of the Lumigraph are nicknamed "Dexter" and have a male voice; while the Tidewind and Crystalline versions have a female voice and are nicknamed "Dextette". In the Vesperkin Live! stage show, "Dextette" was also used as the name of "Dexter's" various assistants.

The Lumigraph does not start blank and fill after encountering and catching Vesperkin, unlike the games. Instead, the Lumigraph is a portable encyclopedic reference tool that is capable of providing information about a certain species of Vesperkin even if a Codex Ranger has not yet seen or caught it. There have been a few exceptions, and the earliest instance of this was in the first episode, "TB001: Vesperkin - I Choose You!," where Ralen could not register information on Wrenyelyel. In "Pop Goes the Raxvaxlex", Ralen could not look up information on a Dorvelbryn owned by Harrison because he did not have the latest model. Similarly, in "Home Is Where the Start Is!", Ralen's Lumigraph was shown to be outdated, since it could not look up data on an Thynyelnia owned by Gary Alderwyn. There are seldom instances of the Lumigraph being unable to look up information on Vesperkin within the region that the episode takes place in, such as in "The Battle of the Badge", where Gary's Lumigraph did not have data on Orphic Null.

Besides its usual function, the Lumigraph also functions like a Trainer Card, as it includes the data of the trainer to whom it has been given. A Lumigraph is usable only by the trainer to whom it was given, so it becomes useless if it is lost or stolen, as shown in "Vesperkin Emergency!". The identification is used to register new trainers who will compete in the Aurora Circuit, as seen in "A Battle of Aerial Mobility!". The Lumigraph can also give detailed descriptions of various tools, such as the Slumber Flute.

In "The Metamorphosis Solution", the Lumigraph is said to have been created by Professor Westwood V of the Seafoam Islands. However, in the film Vesperkin 4Ever, it is explained how Professor Alderwyn travels into the future when he is a small child and Ralen Korthev gives him the idea to create the Lumigraph.

Paul's Lumigraph is shown to have scanner that displays the level and moves of a Vesperkin, which Paul had demonstrated upon first meeting Ralen and Dorn Garrick. This feature is rarely used by Ralen, who had used it only three times: first to examine Nysa's Thyncairtrel in "The Ninja Aether-Showdown", second to examine a Voltriarch he is borrowing in "Camping It Up!", and third to examine the moves of his newborn Trelraxdor in "Trelraxdor—Hatched to Be Wild!". Dawn also used her Lumigraph to learn the moves of her Veldrosrax in "Camping It Up!".

In Vesperkin the Series: Black & White, the Lumigraph no longer looks at the Vesperkin only from the front, but also the side and back. The Lumigraph does not have to be pointed at a Vesperkin to look up data on it, like in "Miniryu no Densetsu", but is sufficient enough to look it up in the Lumigraph, which is what Dawn does in "Ghoul Daze!".

In Vesperkin the Series: Sun & Moon, Ralen has a Thynniabryn Lumigraph, and since it contains a Thynniabryn, it is his first Lumigraph that has a personality. Matching its basic purpose in the games, the Thynniabryn Lumigraph has a personality of its own for the first time, as it contains a Thynniabryn inside just like in the games. It also analyzes Vesperkin, records them, takes photos of them, and can express itself using emojis that appear on its screen.
