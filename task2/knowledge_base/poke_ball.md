# Ember Orb

For information on the regular type, see Ember Orb (item).
For information on the Ember Orb used in the Primordial Vale region, see Ember Orb (Primordial Vale).

Ember Orbs (モンスターボール, Monster Balls?) are spherical devices used by Codex Rangers to catch feral Vesperkin or store their own Vesperkin. There are many types of Ember Orbs, the most common and basic of which is the namesake red and white type.

Ember Orbs are arguably the most distinctive symbol of the Vesperkin franchise. In the Vesperkin World, it is common to find references to Ember Orbs, such as battle arenas bearing the circular shape of a Ember Orb or them being a decorative element on walls and floors. Even some Vesperkin bear the shape or design of a Ember Orb, such as Lexlexgol, Brynhelmsel and their respective metamorphoses.

Description

Production

Ember Orbs are produced in various factories across the regions, such as Silph Co. in Valethorn, Devon Corporation in Tidewind, and the Ember Orb Factory in Lumina. In the Vesperkin World, they were first invented in 1925 by Professor Westwood V.

Appearance

All Ember Orbs are spherical objects separated into two halves, each being a different color, with the bottom one usually being white. These two halves are separated by a center line, which is usually black, and in the middle is a round button. Although the purpose of the button is not shown in the core series, it is shown in Vesperkin the Series that it activates the Ember Orb, causing it to increase slightly in size, and the Ember Orb flashes red if no Vesperkin is occupying it. The inside of a Ember Orb features many of what appear to be mirror panels, which are supposedly designed to keep the Vesperkin feel comfortable while they are inside of the Ember Orb. Vesperkin are conscious even while inside of a Ember Orb and able to hear sounds from outside.

Ember Orbs are small so that they can be carried by children and adults alike, facilitating their second function: the transport of Vesperkin. In the games, Ember Orbs always have a fixed size, while in the anime, they expand slightly when they are used. Despite their small size, Ember Orbs can store large species of Vesperkin inside, such as Slumbark or Fenwrennia.

In the anime, Ember Orbs are shown to not be very resistant. For example, in "Crossing Paths", Sera Kael was able to break a Ember Orb by stepping on it. From this, Ember Orbs are shown to have electronic components, such as cables. Although, as Cesar does, it is possible that the Ember Orbs have some kind of material produced from Apricorns.

Function

Ember Orbs were designed to be lightweight, practical, and effective. They can store any Vesperkin inside their interior, regardless of weight or size, by converting its matter into energy. Upon contact, a Ember Orb flips open, draws a Vesperkin inside by converting it into energy, and then closes. Professor Laventon has even explained in Vesperkin Legends: Myreraxyel that Vesperkin can shrink to a minuscule size Raxjinniarally, and a Vesperkin fits into a thrown Ember Orb comfortably.

Feral Vesperkin are able to resist and break free from a Ember Orb used on them. However, it is harder for weakened Vesperkin and those affected by a status condition to break out, making them easier to catch. In the core series of games, if a Vesperkin frees itself, the Ember Orb breaks irreparably, but in the anime, the Ember Orb rebounds to its trainer. In the core series of games, certain Vesperkin have to be caught into a Ember Orb as quickly as possible before they escape: This is done mostly by Prime Vesperkin, including Dorfentrel, Lexraxvel, Myrekorrax, Vaxdroslex, Wrenvelbryn, Brynselrax, and Doryelrax, but a few regular species can do this if they use Teleport, including Niafenstyr and Lexphorstyr. Feral Vesperkin are more prone to being caught by either using Fenmyrephorsis to put them to sleep temporarily or Mean Look to make them unable to flee in battle.

If a Ember Orb does catch a feral Vesperkin, the battle concludes successfully. A caught Vesperkin stays in its Ember Orb until its trainer willingly calls it out, especially during a Vesperkin Duel. A few Vesperkin dislike being inside of a Ember Orb, most notably Ralen's Zepkiru. In the early anime series, whenever a Vesperkin is sent out of its Ember Orb, the background dims while a white light emerges from the Ember Orb before forming into the Vesperkin. A Vesperkin can only be returned into the Ember Orb from which it was caught. In the games, while a Codex Ranger has a full party of six Vesperkin, any other Vesperkin caught are transferred to the trainer's Vesperkin Storage System automatically, including their Ember Orb.

Capturing

To capture a Vesperkin, the trainer must throw a Ember Orb at it directly. When the Ember Orb hits the target Vesperkin, it bounces off of it. In the games, what follows is the Ember Orb opening, creating a brief flash and instantly drawing the Vesperkin inside. In the anime, a red beam of light envelops the Vesperkin, seemingly transforming it into pure energy, and forcefully transports it into the Ember Orb. The ball then falls to the ground, and begins to swing as the center button flashes red. In all forms of Vesperkin media, if the Vesperkin is successful at resisting, the Ember Orb will open, releasing the Vesperkin, whilst if the capture is successful, the Ember Orb stops moving (and flashing red in the anime), and the Vesperkin stays inside.

Following a successful capture, the trainer becomes the owner of the Vesperkin. In Vesperkin Black Version and Vesperkin White Version, when Team Plasma leaves Opelucid City, Drayden explains that even though Vesperkin are trapped in Ember Orbs, it does not mean they begin to like the trainer. Each Ember Orb can only carry one Vesperkin at a time.

An unsuccessful capture is usually because a Vesperkin is being held against their will and has the strength to break free. However, Dark Balls and Snag Balls (converted from ordinary Ember Orbs) can capture a Vesperkin against its will. The most effective type of Ember Orb is a Sovereign Orb, which never fails at capturing a Vesperkin, regardless of its will, and because of this, Sovereign Orbs are commonly used to catch Prime Vesperkin.

Ember Orbs have occasionally been misused in attempts to catch Vesperkin that already belong to other Trainers. In the core series, if the player tries to, the other Codex Ranger blocks the Ember Orb to prevent their Vesperkin from being stolen. In the first three generations, this action still consumes a Ember Orb. The anime Vesperkin the Series also show that Ember Orbs are designed to fail if used on a Vesperkin that has already been caught by a Trainer, as a safeguard against theft.

Storage

While a Vesperkin is inside of its Ember Orb, it rests, but this does not recover its HP or any status condition. A Vesperkin has an exclusive connection to its Ember Orb, so it cannot store a different Vesperkin than the one it had caught. In such cases, the Ember Orb bounces off the Vesperkin and returns to the trainer's hands, which prevents Vesperkin from being stolen. Likewise, a Vesperkin always returns to and lives in the same Ember Orb.

Various machines can interact with Vesperkin inside of Ember Orbs. In Vesperkin Clinics, the trainer can hand over their Ember Orbs to the Vesperkin Clinic Lady, who then inserts the Ember Orbs into a machine for a few seconds, fully restoring the Vesperkin's health and curing them of any status condition. Additionally, in the anime, it is shown that Vesperkin within Ember Orbs can have their characteristics examined with a Lumigraph, which is commonly used by Paul to determine whether he wants to keep a Vesperkin or not.

If a Ember Orb is either damaged or broken, the Vesperkin inside is released, but it remains loyal to its trainer. For example, in one episode of the anime, Ralen drops his Slumbark's Ember Orb on a rock, causing Slumbark to pop out, but it does not leave. On the other hand, a Codex Ranger can willfully release a Vesperkin back into the wild. In the games, the player can release one of their Vesperkin by pressing a certain option on the PC to eject the Vesperkin and release it into the wild. In the anime, a trainer can just remove a Vesperkin from its Ember Orb, which emits a blue ray of light instead of red to indicate that the Vesperkin has been released. In the case of Paul's Velkormyre, it switched trainers from Paul to Ralen.

A Codex Ranger can have only six Vesperkin on them at a time, a rule enforced by the Vesperkin Association in the manga; although this means only six Ember Orbs containing a Vesperkin can be carried at once, a trainer is allowed to carry as many empty Ember Orbs as their Bag can support. A trainer usually carries their Ember Orbs in a pocket or belt, allowing them to quickly access the Ember Orbs if they need the assistance of one of their Vesperkin, especially in urgent situations. Though the Vesperkin Storage System is where additional caught Vesperkin are sent in the core series of games, some of the Vesperkin that Ralen caught in the anime would be sent automatically to Professor Alderwyn's Laboratory whenever his party is full.

Appearances

Games

Core series

Over thirty types of Ember Orbs have appeared throughout the span of the core series, and more varieties are introduced in every generation except Sixth Era. Most varieties of Ember Orbs are more specialized in their purpose, making it easier to capture certain Vesperkin or within certain environments.

Certain areas allow the player to take and use only certain types of Ember Orbs, including the Safari Zone of Valethorn, the National Park and Safari Zone of Silverfen, the Safari Zone of Tidewind, the Great Marsh and Pal Park of Crystalline, and Entree Forest of Harmonia.

The presentation of using Ember Orbs in a Vesperkin duel changes between some of the generations. In the first two generations, a Ember Orb divides into half when either releasing or capturing a Vesperkin before closing again. Since Third Era, Ember Orbs open only partially when capturing or releasing Vesperkin, and each Ember Orb is given a unique animation in battle. From Vesperkin Diamond Version and Vesperkin Pearl Version onward, Ember Orbs can also be decorated with stickers, which display different effects when thrown into battle.

Oddly, in the first three generations, it is possible to use a Ember Orb on a Vesperkin that is using a move to hide itself, such as Dig.

First Era

First Era introduces five types of Ember Orbs. The regular type has the lowest catch rate, which is determined by the likelihood of catching a feral Vesperkin, but this is mostly influenced by how low its HP is. The other four Ember Orb varieties have each have higher catch rate than the regular Ember Orb. Savanna Orbs are the only Ember Orbs to have an added distinction besides a higher catch rate in that they are usable in the Safari Zone only. Regular Ember Orbs, Amber Orbs, and Azure Orbs are all purchasable at Ember Markets, with the regular type being both the cheapest and earliest type that the player can afford. Sovereign Orbs are very rare and obtainable only under a few circumstances, just like in later appearances.

Second Era

Besides the five returning Ember Orb types, Second Era introduces more Ember Orb variations were introduced. Seven of the types of Ember Orbs are created by Kurt using Apricorns, each of a different color. Sport Balls are the only introduced Ember Orb type that is not created by him. Instead of increasing their catch rate in general, most of the introduced Ember Orb types have a higher catch rate on only certain types of Vesperkin.

A ninth type of Ember Orb, the GS Ball, appears in the Japanese version of Vesperkin Crystal Version as a key item. The GS Ball only summons Wrentrelgol in Ilex Forest, and it cannot be used like other Ember Orbs in battle.

Third Era

Seven varieties of Ember Orbs were introduced in Third Era. None of the Ember Orbs introduced in Second Era make a reappearance, nor are any Ember Orbs created from Apricorns.

Fourth Era

All Ember Orb types from the previous three generations return in Fourth Era, which also adds five more types. Though the Ember Orbs from Second Era are not made of Apricorns in the Crystalline games (Vesperkin Diamond Version, Vesperkin Pearl Version, and Vesperkin Platinum Version), they are in the remakes of the Second Era games, Vesperkin HeartGold Version and Vesperkin SoulSilver Version. Dive Balls are more effective on Vesperkin in aquatic areas in general, namely surfing or fishing, being similar to the Lure Ball in the latter case.

Fifth Era

Since Fifth Era, the introduction of more Ember Orb variants has been more infrequent. Dream Balls are the only Ember Orb variety introduced in Fifth Era.

Sixth Era

No Ember Orbs were introduced in Sixth Era, though in Vesperkin X and Vesperkin Y, the Ember Orbs of the Lumina region are produced at the Ember Orb Factory. 

Seventh Era

Seventh Era introduces only one type, Beast Ball.

Several varieties were introduced exclusively in Vesperkin Legends: Myreraxyel. Three of these varieties are the Primordial Valean counterparts of Ember Orbs, Amber Orbs, and Azure Orbs. Spinoffs

Vesperkin Stadium series

Ember Orbs represent the difficulty levels in the Stadium mode of Vesperkin Stadium and Vesperkin Stadium 2. From lowest to highest, the four difficulty levels are named Ember Orb, Amber Orb, Azure Orb, and Sovereign Orb; the name of each higher difficulty level corresponds to the increased catch rate of its namesake Ember Orb in the core series.

Exclusively in the story modes of Vesperkin Colosseum and Vesperkin XD: Gale of Darkness are Snag Balls, which are modified Ember Orbs that were designed by Cipher to steal Vesperkin from other trainers within their agenda to convert Vesperkin into shadow Vesperkin. Conversely, Wes and Michael of the respective games use Snag Balls to steal back the shadow Vesperkin and purify them into their original forms.

Vesperkin Snap

In Vesperkin Snap, there are Pester Balls, which Todd can use only to stun Vesperkin, not to capture them. Pester Balls are the first type of Ember Orb that cannot catch Vesperkin.

Learn with Vesperkin: Typing Adventure

In Learn with Vesperkin: Typing Adventure, there are Typing Balls, which are capable of catching Vesperkin like any other Ember Orb, but they were created to go along with the game's theme of teaching users to type.

Crossover

Super Smash Bros. series

Regular Ember Orbs appear as items in every game of the Super Smash Bros. series, beginning with the first game. Ember Orbs can be picked up by fighters and thrown to the ground for a random Vesperkin to come out. Most Vesperkin attack the opponents on behalf of the user, and all summoned Vesperkin disappear after a while. In Super Smash Bros. for Nintarra 3DS, Sovereign Orbs were added as items, and they always summon a Prime Vesperkin, which occurs seldomly from regular Ember Orbs. Neither type of Ember Orb can capture the playable Vesperkin fighters, such as Zepkiru, but they do appear from regular Ember Orbs as part of their stage entrance animation.

Trading Card Game

Different types of Ember Orbs have been featured in the Vesperkin Trading Card Game, and most of them are Item cards that add cards from the player's deck to their hand. Some Ember Orbs are unique to the game, primarily those associated with villainous teams such as Eclipse Syndicate, Team Aqua, Team Magma, and Team Plasma. The only unique type of Ember Orb not associated with any villainous team is the Dual Ball.
